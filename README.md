## Overview

Large language models are increasingly used for conversations involving personal and emotional concerns. But when someone communicates possible signs of depression, **can an LLM recognize the relevant cues and respond appropriately?**

This ongoing research project investigates how general-purpose LLMs interpret and respond to depression-related information emerging in simulated conversations. It considers not only recognition of potentially important cues, but also the broader quality and appropriateness of conversational responses.

The study is developed with **clinical input from a specialist in psychiatry** and aims to contribute to evidence-based discussion of the opportunities, limitations, and safety considerations of LLMs in mental-health-related interactions.

> **Scope:** This is an evaluation of research systems in simulated interactions. It is not a clinical diagnostic tool or a substitute for professional mental healthcare.

## Research focus

The project explores three high-level questions:

- **Cue recognition:** How effectively do LLMs recognize depression-related signals conveyed during a conversation?
- **Conversational response:** How appropriately do models respond to potentially sensitive mental-health disclosures?
- **Model comparison:** What similarities and differences emerge across widely used conversational LLM systems?

## Clinical collaboration and simulation

A key part of the work involves **clinician-informed simulated patient interactions** to support the study of mental-health conversations. Clinical expertise informs the research design and assessment of model behavior.

The work emphasizes responsible interpretation: useful conversational behavior should not be confused with an established ability to diagnose or treat depression.
